1
Basic concepts

Define the parts before using the methods

These terms form the shared language for every later rule.

sentence
a grammatically complete statement or question
Example: The survey reveals a consistent pattern.
clause
a word group containing a subject and a verb
Example: The survey reveals a consistent pattern
independent clause (IC)
a clause that can stand alone as a sentence
Example: The archive reopened in May.
dependent clause (DC)
a clause that cannot stand alone because a word such as although, because, or when makes it dependent
Example: Although the archive reopened in May
subject
the noun or pronoun that controls a finite verb
Example: The collection of maps is extensive.
head subject
the central noun or pronoun inside the complete subject
Example: collection in the collection of maps
verb
a word or phrase that expresses an action, event, or state
Example: measured, has changed, is
finite verb
a verb marked for tense or agreement that can serve as a clause's main verb
Example: The instrument records temperature.
nonfinite verb
an infinitive, gerund, or participle that does not by itself serve as a tense-marked main verb
Example: to record, recording, recorded by the team
number
the singular or plural status of a noun, pronoun, or verb
Example: sample is; samples are
tense
the grammatical form that locates an event in time
Example: worked, works, will work
aspect
the form that shows an event as ongoing, completed, or connected to another time
Example: is working, has worked, had worked
pronoun
a word that replaces or points to a noun phrase
Example: it, they, them, their, which
antecedent
the noun phrase to which a pronoun refers
Example: the engineers in The engineers revised their model
case
the pronoun form required by its role as subject, object, or possessor
Example: she, her, hers
modifier
a word, phrase, or clause that describes or limits another element
Example: Using radar in Using radar, the team mapped the coast.
parallel structure
matching grammatical forms for ideas with equal logical roles
Example: to collect, to compare, and to report
comparison
a statement that places like kinds or qualities against each other
Example: The new lens is clearer than the old lens.
possessive
a form that shows ownership or another belonging relationship
Example: the scientist's notes; the scientists' notes
contraction
a shortened form that combines words and uses an apostrophe
Example: it's means it is or it has
logical relationship
the exact way one idea develops, opposes, explains, results from, or follows another
Example: obstacle plus surviving result equals concession
transition
a word or phrase that makes a logical relationship explicit
Example: however, specifically, therefore, meanwhile
coordinating conjunction
one of for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so; these seven words are remembered as FANBOYS
Example: The trial was small, but its result was consistent.
subordinating conjunction
a word such as although, because, if, when, or while that makes a clause dependent
Example: Although the trial was small, its result was consistent.
conjunctive adverb
a movable transition such as however, therefore, or meanwhile that cannot by itself join two independent clauses
Example: The trial was small; however, its result was consistent.
answer-like form
a resolved word form, transition, or sentence pattern that resembles what a correct SAT option would supply without reproducing a full question
Example: head subject plus matching finite verb
2
Standard English Conventions

Form, Structure, and Sense

Choose the form controlled by sentence structure, time, reference, ownership, matched roles, and logical meaning.

1

1. A clause needs the right finite-verb structure

Concept / Rule

A clause needs a subject and a finite verb. An -ing form, an infinitive, or a participle may belong in a phrase, but it cannot automatically fill the main-verb slot.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which form completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: fragments is the plural head subject; the clause needs a tense-marked main verb before the past action compared.

The ceramic fragments recovered from the harbor (catalog) were cataloged before the research team compared their mineral content.

Answer-like form

subject ceramic fragments + finite verb were cataloged; nonfinite modifier recovered from the harbor

Explanation

Were cataloged supplies tense and agrees with the plural head subject fragments. Recovered modifies fragments; it is not a second main verb.

Closest trap

Mistake: The ceramic fragments recovered from the harbor, being cataloged before the team compared them.

Failure: Being cataloged leaves the main clause without a finite verb.

Repair: The ceramic fragments recovered from the harbor were cataloged before the team compared them.

Portable rule: Name every clause and mark its finite verb before judging an action-looking form.

2

2. The head subject controls agreement, not the nearest noun

Concept / Rule

A finite verb agrees in number with the head subject. Prepositional phrases, appositives, and other interruptions do not replace that controller.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which form completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: collection is the singular head subject; of eighteenth-century maps is an interrupter.

A collection of eighteenth-century maps (be) is stored in the climate-controlled room.

Answer-like form

head subject collection = singular; finite verb is = singular

Explanation

Maps is plural but appears inside the phrase of eighteenth-century maps. Collection remains the singular head subject.

Closest trap

Mistake: A collection of eighteenth-century maps are stored in the climate-controlled room.

Failure: The verb incorrectly agrees with the nearby plural noun maps instead of the singular head subject collection.

Repair: A collection of eighteenth-century maps is stored in the climate-controlled room.

Portable rule: Cross out interrupters, then match the finite verb to the head subject.

3

3. Compound, indefinite, and inverted subjects still have exact controllers

Concept / Rule

Subjects joined by and are usually plural; subjects joined by or or nor agree with the nearer subject. Singular indefinite pronouns such as each and neither take singular verbs. In inverted order, the subject may follow the verb.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which forms complete the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: each is singular; statues is a postverb plural subject; with neither...nor, the nearer subject catalog controls the final verb.

Three patterns control agreement: each of the field notebooks (contain) contains a date; near the entrance (stand) stand two restored statues; and neither the photographs nor the catalog (provide) provides the missing coordinates.

Answer-like form

each = singular, so contains; postverb subject statues = plural, so stand; nearer subject catalog = singular, so provides

Explanation

Singular each controls contains despite notebooks. In the inverted middle clause, plural statues follows but still controls stand. With neither nor, the nearer singular catalog controls provides.

Closest trap

Mistake: Each of the field notebooks contain a date; near the entrance stands two restored statues; and neither the photographs nor the catalog provide the missing coordinates.

Failure: Each is singular, inverted order does not change the plural subject statues, and the nearer singular subject catalog controls the final verb.

Repair: Each of the field notebooks contains a date; near the entrance stand two restored statues; and neither the photographs nor the catalog provides the missing coordinates.

Portable rule: Identify the connector and exact subject before assigning number.

4

4. Tense and aspect express a time relationship

Concept / Rule

Choose tense and aspect from explicit time anchors and event order. Simple forms locate an event; progressive forms emphasize duration; perfect forms place one event before or up to a reference time.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which form completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: by the time and opened in 2024 create a past reference point; restoration was already complete before that point.

By the time the museum opened its new wing in 2024, conservators (restore) had restored all twelve murals.

Answer-like form

past perfect had restored = restoration completed before past reference event opened

Explanation

By the time establishes two past events and makes restoration earlier than the opening. Past perfect marks that earlier completed event.

Closest trap

Mistake: By the time the museum opened its new wing in 2024, conservators restore all twelve murals.

Failure: Present tense restore conflicts with the completed past timeline.

Repair: By the time the museum opened its new wing in 2024, conservators had restored all twelve murals.

Portable rule: Build a two-event timeline; use perfect aspect only when a reference time justifies it.

5

5. A pronoun needs a clear antecedent, matching features, and correct case

Concept / Rule

A pronoun must point clearly to an antecedent and match it in number and person. Its form must also fit its role as subject, object, or possessor.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice supplies pronouns that conform to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: engineers is plural; asked requires an object; changes belong to the engineers.

A. they / its B. them / their C. they / their D. them / its

After the engineers revised the prototype, the director asked them to document their changes.

Answer-like form

them = plural object referring to engineers; their = plural possessive referring to engineers

Explanation

The director asked the engineers, so object-case them is required. The changes belong to the engineers, so possessive their is required.

Closest trap

Mistake: After the engineers revised the prototype, the director asked they to document its changes.

Failure: They has subject case in an object slot, and its does not agree with the plural antecedent engineers.

Repair: After the engineers revised the prototype, the director asked them to document their changes.

Portable rule: Replace each pronoun with its antecedent, then check number, person, and sentence role.

6

6. A modifier must sit beside its logical target

Concept / Rule

An opening, inserted, or final modifier must clearly describe the intended noun. An introductory participial phrase normally modifies the subject that immediately follows it.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: the noun immediately after the opening modifier must be able to use radar.

A. channels beneath the glacier B. the radar pulses C. the research team D. a detailed map

Using high-resolution radar, the research team mapped channels beneath the glacier.

Answer-like form

opening modifier Using high-resolution radar -> adjacent logical subject the research team

Explanation

The research team can logically use radar, so the opening modifier attaches to a capable actor.

Closest trap

Mistake: Using high-resolution radar, channels beneath the glacier were mapped by the research team.

Failure: The sentence makes channels the grammatical target of Using high-resolution radar even though channels cannot use radar.

Repair: Using high-resolution radar, the research team mapped channels beneath the glacier.

Portable rule: After an opening modifier, name the actor immediately.

7

7. Modifier form and position must preserve meaning

Concept / Rule

Adjectives modify nouns or pronouns; adverbs usually modify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs, or whole clauses. Moving only or almost can change scope even when every word remains grammatical.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which forms complete the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English and preserves the intended scope?

Evidence to inspect: the first modifiers describe how detection occurred; only must sit beside the damaged valve to exclude other valves.

The sensor detected the pressure change (surprising / quick) surprisingly quickly , and the team tested only the damaged valve.

Answer-like form

surprisingly modifies quickly; quickly modifies detected; only limits the damaged valve

Explanation

The adverb chain describes how detection occurred. Only sits next to the noun phrase it limits, so the test excluded other valves rather than other actions.

Closest trap

Mistake: The sensor detected the pressure change surprising quick, and the team only tested the damaged valve.

Failure: Surprising quick uses adjective forms where adverbs are needed, and only ambiguously limits tested rather than the damaged valve.

Repair: The sensor detected the pressure change surprisingly quickly, and the team tested only the damaged valve.

Portable rule: Choose modifier form by its target, then place limiting words beside the exact idea they limit.

8

8. Apostrophes show possession or contraction, not ordinary plurality

Concept / Rule

Form an ordinary plural without an apostrophe. For possession, place the apostrophe according to the number of owners. Expand a contraction before choosing it.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice supplies the three forms that conform to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: two biologists jointly own the notes; one lead biologist owns the notebook; the last clause must expand to it is unclear.

A. biologists’ / biologist’s / it’s B. biologist’s / biologists’ / its C. biologists / biologist’s / its D. biologists’ / biologists / it’s

The two biologists ’ field notes were stored beside the lead biologist ’s notebook, but it’s unclear when the files were digitized.

Answer-like form

biologists’ = plural owners; biologist’s = one owner; it’s = it is

Explanation

The first notes belong to two biologists, so the apostrophe follows plural s. The notebook belongs to one biologist. It’s expands to it is and fits the clause.

Closest trap

Mistake: The two biologist’s field notes were stored beside the lead biologists notebook, but its unclear when the files were digitized.

Failure: The first form marks one owner instead of two, the second omits possession, and its cannot expand to it is.

Repair: The two biologists’ field notes were stored beside the lead biologist’s notebook, but it’s unclear when the files were digitized.

Portable rule: Count owners, identify the owned noun, and expand every possible contraction.

9

9. Equal logical roles require parallel grammatical forms

Concept / Rule

Items in a list, coordinated actions, paired constructions, and comparisons must match at the same structural level. Repeated topic words do not repair mismatched grammar.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which forms complete the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: all three coordinated phrases complete aims and name equal project goals.

The project aims (recover) to recover the recordings, (verify) to verify their dates, and (publish) to publish a searchable catalog.

Answer-like form

to recover + to verify + to publish = three parallel infinitive phrases

Explanation

All three phrases complete aims and name equal project goals in the same grammatical form.

Closest trap

Mistake: The project aims to recover the recordings, verifying their dates, and to publish a searchable catalog.

Failure: The middle gerund form breaks the matched infinitive series.

Repair: The project aims to recover the recordings, to verify their dates, and to publish a searchable catalog.

Portable rule: Bracket peer items and give every equal role the same grammatical label.

10

10. Logical comparisons and sentence sense must compare like with like

Concept / Rule

A comparison must name equivalent categories and preserve the intended agent, object, and scope. A grammatically smooth sentence can still be illogical.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Evidence to inspect: compare the new microscope’s resolution with the older microscope’s resolution, not with the microscope itself.

A. the older microscope B. that of the laboratory’s older microscope C. those of the laboratory’s older microscope D. the laboratory’s older one

The resolution of the new microscope is greater than that of the laboratory’s older microscope.

Answer-like form

resolution of new microscope compared with resolution of older microscope; that substitutes for resolution

Explanation

That of supplies the matching quality, so resolution is compared with resolution rather than with a physical microscope.

Closest trap

Mistake: The resolution of the new microscope is greater than the laboratory’s older microscope.

Failure: The sentence compares a resolution with a microscope, two unlike categories.

Repair: The resolution of the new microscope is greater than that of the laboratory’s older microscope.

Portable rule: State both sides of the comparison in full; if their categories differ, repair the sentence.

4
Expression of Ideas

Transitions

Name the relationship first, then choose the exact discourse job and legal grammatical form.

1

1. Map the relationship before choosing the connector

Concept / Rule

A transition names the relationship between complete ideas. Summarize each side first, then state the relationship in plain language before considering a specific word.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: the first sentence limits the survey; the second preserves a consistent result despite that limit.

A. Nevertheless, B. Additionally, C. Similarly, D. For example,

The initial survey covered only four wetlands. Nevertheless, the same population pattern appeared at every site.

Answer-like form

Idea 1 = limitation; Idea 2 = result survives limitation; relation = concession; connector = nevertheless

Explanation

The second idea does not deny the small sample. It acknowledges that limitation while preserving the result’s value.

Closest trap

Mistake: The initial survey covered only four wetlands. Additionally, the same population pattern appeared at every site.

Failure: Additionally marks simple accumulation and loses the obstacle-plus-surviving-result relationship.

Repair: The initial survey covered only four wetlands. Nevertheless, the same population pattern appeared at every site.

Portable rule: Replace the transition with a plain relationship phrase before testing vocabulary.

2

2. Same direction still contains different jobs

Concept / Rule

Addition supplies another point; reinforcement strengthens one; restatement recasts it; summary compresses it; comparison shows a parallel. These words may point in the same direction without being interchangeable.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: repairability is a new supporting benefit that strengthens the earlier benefit; it does not restate it.

A. Similarly, B. In other words, C. For example, D. Moreover,

The instrument is inexpensive to manufacture. Moreover, it can be repaired with standard laboratory tools.

Answer-like form

new supporting benefit + stronger accumulation = moreover

Explanation

The second sentence adds a distinct benefit that strengthens the case for the instrument; it does not restate or summarize the first benefit.

Closest trap

Mistake: The instrument is inexpensive to manufacture. In other words, it can be repaired with standard laboratory tools.

Failure: In other words promises a restatement, but repairability is a new benefit rather than a rephrasing of low cost.

Repair: The instrument is inexpensive to manufacture. Moreover, it can be repaired with standard laboratory tools.

Portable rule: Inside same direction, label the second idea as new point, reinforcement, restatement, summary, or comparison.

3

3. Example, specification, clarification, and emphasis develop a claim differently

Concept / Rule

For example gives one case; specifically narrows a broad category to a named detail; in other words clarifies by restating; indeed or in fact emphasizes or confirms.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: the first sentence names an exact measurement category; the second supplies its precise value.

A. In short, B. Specifically, C. For example, D. Meanwhile,

The archive has recorded the exact duration of its volunteer-produced radio drama holdings. Specifically, those holdings comprise 72 hours and 14 minutes of material.

Answer-like form

measurement category exact duration -> precise value 72 hours and 14 minutes = specifically

Explanation

The second sentence specifies the exact value of the duration named in the first; it is not one example from a larger set.

Closest trap

Mistake: The archive has recorded the exact duration of its volunteer-produced radio drama holdings. In short, those holdings comprise 72 hours and 14 minutes of material.

Failure: In short signals summary, but the second sentence supplies the exact measurement requested by the first.

Repair: The archive has recorded the exact duration of its volunteer-produced radio drama holdings. Specifically, those holdings comprise 72 hours and 14 minutes of material.

Portable rule: Ask whether the second idea is one case, an exact detail, a restatement, or an emphasis.

4

4. Contrast, contradiction, and concession preserve different claim directions

Concept / Rule

General contrast marks difference; contradiction presents an unexpected surviving result; concession acknowledges an obstacle while preserving the main claim.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: the materials differ in nickel content but match in corrosion resistance; the second fact does not result from the first.

A. Therefore, B. Likewise, C. However, D. For example,

The alloy contains less nickel than the standard material. However, both alloys resist corrosion equally well.

Answer-like form

difference between composition and performance expectation = general contrast with however

Explanation

The second fact contrasts with an expected consequence of lower nickel but does not replace the first claim.

Closest trap

Mistake: The alloy contains less nickel than the standard material. Therefore, both alloys resist corrosion equally well.

Failure: Therefore asserts that lower nickel causes equal corrosion resistance, a causal link the sentences do not support.

Repair: The alloy contains less nickel than the standard material. However, both alloys resist corrosion equally well.

Portable rule: Name both propositions and preserve which one remains central after the contrast.

5

5. Replacement, correction, alternative, and contrary claims are not generic contrast

Concept / Rule

Instead or rather replaces or corrects one path; alternatively offers another option; on the contrary rejects a prior negative claim and asserts its opposite.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: the first sentence rejects method A; the second substitutes method B.

A. Rather, B. Nevertheless, C. Similarly, D. Consequently,

The team did not date the pigment itself. Rather, it dated the mineral crust that had formed over the image.

Answer-like form

not method A; correction to method B = rather

Explanation

The second sentence corrects the method described in the first sentence; it is not merely a contrasting fact.

Closest trap

Mistake: The team did not date the pigment itself. Nevertheless, it dated the mineral crust that had formed over the image.

Failure: Nevertheless suggests the second action occurred despite an obstacle, not that it replaces the first method.

Repair: The team did not date the pigment itself. Rather, it dated the mineral crust that had formed over the image.

Portable rule: When the first idea rejects one path, test whether the second supplies its replacement.

6

6. Cause, effect, inference, and purpose require the correct direction

Concept / Rule

Because introduces a cause; therefore or consequently introduces a result or supported inference; to this end introduces an action taken toward a stated goal. Related events do not automatically create causation.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: freezing expands water in the cracks; widening is the resulting physical change.

A. For example, B. Meanwhile, C. Nevertheless, D. Consequently,

Repeated freezing expanded the water inside the rock’s cracks. Consequently, the cracks widened over several winters.

Answer-like form

cause = repeated freezing and expansion; effect = widening cracks; connector = consequently

Explanation

The first sentence supplies the process that produces the later change, so the causal direction runs from expansion to widening.

Closest trap

Mistake: Repeated freezing expanded the water inside the rock’s cracks. For example, the cracks widened over several winters.

Failure: Widening is the result of the process, not merely one member of a category.

Repair: Repeated freezing expanded the water inside the rock’s cracks. Consequently, the cracks widened over several winters.

Portable rule: Label cause, effect, goal, action, and inference before choosing a connector.

7

7. Time and sequence transitions need an explicit timeline

Concept / Rule

Previously, subsequently, meanwhile, eventually, and ultimately encode different temporal relations. Tense alone does not justify one; the text must establish earlier, later, simultaneous, delayed, or final status.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: calibration lasted from 9:00 to 11:00; installation ran from 9:30 to 10:30, so the intervals overlap.

A. Eventually, B. Meanwhile, C. Previously, D. Consequently,

The team calibrated the sensors from 9:00 to 11:00 a.m. Meanwhile, a second group installed the receivers from 9:30 to 10:30 a.m.

Answer-like form

9:00–11:00 overlaps 9:30–10:30 = meanwhile

Explanation

The two time intervals explicitly overlap, so simultaneity rather than simple addition or later sequence controls the transition.

Closest trap

Mistake: The team calibrated the sensors in the morning. Eventually, a second group installed the receivers at the field site.

Failure: Eventually suggests delay or a final later outcome that the sentence does not establish.

Repair: The team calibrated the sensors in the morning. Meanwhile, a second group installed the receivers at the field site.

Portable rule: Place both events on a timeline and name their gap before selecting a time word.

8

8. Fittingly marks appropriateness, not cause or ordinary example

Concept / Rule

Fittingly or appropriately shows that a fact suits an earlier name, role, purpose, design, or circumstance. The match must be meaningful, not merely topical.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Evidence to inspect: the leaf design suits the arboretum’s preservation purpose; the text does not claim direct causation.

A. Consequently, B. For example, C. Fittingly, D. Meanwhile,

The city’s arboretum was founded to preserve rare trees. Fittingly, its entrance gate is carved with silhouettes of native leaves.

Answer-like form

arboretum purpose rare-tree preservation matches leaf design at entrance = fittingly

Explanation

The gate’s design appropriately echoes the institution’s purpose; the preservation mission is not presented as a direct cause of the carving.

Closest trap

Mistake: The city’s arboretum was founded to preserve rare trees. Consequently, its entrance gate is carved with silhouettes of native leaves.

Failure: Consequently overstates the appropriateness match as a direct causal result.

Repair: The city’s arboretum was founded to preserve rare trees. Fittingly, its entrance gate is carved with silhouettes of native leaves.

Portable rule: Use fittingly only when the second fact is especially suitable in light of the first.

9

9. Transition meaning and grammatical form are separate decisions

Concept / Rule

A conjunctive adverb cannot join two independent clauses with only a comma. Use a period or semicolon before it and usually a comma after it. A coordinating conjunction or subordinating conjunction follows a different clause rule.

Resolved SAT-style micro-prompt

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition and correct punctuation?

Evidence to inspect: both sides are independent clauses; the second contrasts this species with related species.

A. ; however, B. , however, C. ; therefore, D. , but,

Most related species burrow beneath the sand ; however , this species remains on the surface.

Answer-like form

IC ; conjunctive adverb , IC. The semicolon closes the first IC; however names contrast; the comma closes the transition.

Explanation

Both sides are independent clauses, so the boundary must be strong. However supplies meaning but does not itself create the legal join.

Closest trap

Mistake: Most related species burrow beneath the sand, however, this species remains on the surface.

Failure: A comma plus however creates a comma splice between independent clauses.

Repair: Most related species burrow beneath the sand; however, this species remains on the surface.

Portable rule: Solve clause boundary and transition meaning as two separate fields.

Permission map

Logical and grammatical transition permissions

Meaning and sentence form remain separate checks.

Permission Complete legal sentence Complete illegal sentence Controlling rule
Addition Legal The archive preserves letters from the expedition. Moreover, it contains the crew’s field sketches. Illegal The archive preserves letters from the expedition. In other words, it contains the crew’s field sketches. A new supporting detail is not a restatement.
Specification Legal The collection includes several marine fossils. Specifically, it contains three complete ammonite shells. Illegal The collection includes several marine fossils. In short, it contains three complete ammonite shells. A named subset is specification, not summary.
Concession Legal The pilot study was small. Nevertheless, its pattern was consistent across all four sites. Illegal The pilot study was small. Therefore, its pattern was consistent across all four sites. A surviving result despite a limit is not caused by the limit.
Replacement Legal The team did not test the pigment. Rather, it tested the mineral layer above it. Illegal The team did not test the pigment. Additionally, it tested the mineral layer above it. Not A followed by B signals correction or replacement.
Effect Legal The valve remained closed. Consequently, pressure increased inside the chamber. Illegal The valve remained closed. For example, pressure increased inside the chamber. The second event is a result, not an example.
Purpose Legal The archive sought to reduce handling of fragile pages. To this end, it created high-resolution digital copies. Illegal The archive sought to reduce handling of fragile pages. Meanwhile, it created high-resolution digital copies. The action is taken toward the stated goal.
Simultaneous time Legal One group calibrated the sensors. Meanwhile, another group installed the receivers. Illegal One group calibrated the sensors. Eventually, another group installed the receivers. Meanwhile requires overlap; eventually requires a later endpoint or delay.
Appropriateness Legal The award honors a wave researcher. Fittingly, its trophy resembles a cresting wave. Illegal The award honors a wave researcher. Consequently, its trophy resembles a cresting wave. A suitable match is not automatically a causal effect.
Conjunctive-adverb punctuation Legal The sample was small ; however , the result was consistent. Illegal The sample was small, however, the result was consistent. Two independent clauses need a period or semicolon before however.
Subordinating conjunction Legal Although the sample was small , the result was consistent. Illegal Although the sample was small. The result was consistent. Although makes its clause dependent and attaches it to the independent clause.
Coordinating conjunction Legal The sample was small , but the result was consistent. Illegal The sample was small but, the result was consistent. Comma plus but joins two independent clauses; the comma belongs before but.

Three complete decision routes

Change–Control–Consistency

Resolved demonstration

A sequence of short observations (reveal) reveals how the glacier (shift) has shifted since 1950.

Answer-like form: reveals agrees with singular sequence; has shifted connects past change to the present reference point since 1950

  1. Compare changing forms: verb number and tense/aspect.
  2. Locate controllers: head subject sequence; time anchor since 1950.
  3. State requirements: singular finite verb; present-perfect relation continuing to now.
  4. Resolve forms: reveals; has shifted.
  5. Check sentence sense: observations reveal a continuing historical change.
  6. Reread the complete sentence for grammar and meaning.

Closest trap: Do not let nearby plural observations control reveals, and do not choose simple past if the sentence keeps the time window open to the present.

Relationship–Then–Connector

Resolved demonstration

The restoration removed several later paint layers. As a result, the original geometric pattern became visible.

Answer-like form: Idea 1 = removal; Idea 2 = effect; relation = cause to result; connector = as a result

  1. Summarize before: later paint layers were removed.
  2. Summarize after: original pattern became visible.
  3. Name relation: the second event results from the first.
  4. Reject wrong families: no contrast, example, replacement, or time-only relation.
  5. Choose exact job: result connector as a result.
  6. Reread the pair and confirm causal direction.

Closest trap: For example would mislabel the visible pattern as a case; meanwhile would claim only simultaneous timing.

Boundary + Relationship two-field check

Resolved demonstration

The preliminary model predicted a decline ; however , the final measurements showed no change.

Answer-like form: IC ; however , IC. Boundary = semicolon; relationship = contrast; transition punctuation = following comma.

  1. Classify both sides as independent clauses.
  2. Choose a legal strong boundary: semicolon or period.
  3. Map the relationship: prediction contrasts with measurement.
  4. Choose however for general contrast.
  5. Place a comma after the conjunctive adverb.
  6. Reread for both structural legality and logical precision.

Closest trap: A comma before however cannot join the two independent clauses.

7
High-frequency trap library

Mistake first, then diagnosis and repair

Every trap begins with the complete wrong sentence.

1. Nearest-noun agreement

Mistake: The series of experiments demonstrate the pattern.

Failure: The plural experiments sits inside an interrupting phrase; singular series controls the verb.

Repair: The series of experiments demonstrates the pattern.

Portable rule: Match the finite verb to the head subject.

2. Action word without a finite verb

Mistake: The research team mapping the coastline during three winter surveys.

Failure: Mapping is nonfinite, so the independent clause has no finite main verb.

Repair: The research team mapped the coastline during three winter surveys.

Portable rule: An action meaning does not guarantee a finite verb.

3. Perfect tense without a reference point

Mistake: Last week, the museum has opened its new gallery.

Failure: A finished past time normally calls for simple past; no present reference justifies present perfect.

Repair: Last week, the museum opened its new gallery.

Portable rule: Use perfect aspect only when the timeline supplies its reference relation.

4. Ambiguous pronoun

Mistake: When Maya spoke with Lena, she described the revised model.

Failure: She could refer to Maya or Lena, so the antecedent is not clear.

Repair: When Maya spoke with Lena, Maya described the revised model.

Portable rule: Repeat the noun when two plausible antecedents compete.

5. Dangling modifier

Mistake: After reviewing the scans, several defects were visible.

Failure: Several defects did not review the scans; the logical actor is missing after the modifier.

Repair: After reviewing the scans, the engineers identified several defects.

Portable rule: Put the logical actor directly after an opening modifier.

6. Plural apostrophe trap

Mistake: Three shelter’s roofs were repaired.

Failure: Three makes shelters plural before possession is added.

Repair: Three shelters’ roofs were repaired.

Portable rule: Form the plural first, then mark possession.

7. False parallelism

Mistake: The team valued accuracy, working efficiently, and clear reports.

Failure: The three objects of valued use a noun, a gerund phrase, and a noun phrase with mismatched form.

Repair: The team valued accurate measurements, efficient work, and clear reports.

Portable rule: Match grammatical form across equal logical roles.

8. Wrong transition family

Mistake: The first trial failed to detect an effect. Similarly, the larger trial produced a clear effect.

Failure: The results oppose rather than parallel each other.

Repair: The first trial failed to detect an effect. By contrast, the larger trial produced a clear effect.

Portable rule: Map the relationship before reading connector meanings.

9. Cause reversed

Mistake: The chamber pressure rose. Therefore, the valve had remained closed.

Failure: Without evidence that pressure proves valve position, the sentence may reverse or overclaim the causal direction.

Repair: The valve remained closed. Consequently, the chamber pressure rose.

Portable rule: Name cause and effect before choosing a result connector.

10. Specification treated as summary

Mistake: The archive includes several kinds of oral history. In short, it preserves interviews with dockworkers.

Failure: The interview collection is one precise subset, not a summary of the whole category.

Repair: The archive includes several kinds of oral history. Specifically, it preserves interviews with dockworkers.

Portable rule: Use specifically for broad-to-narrow development.

11. Fittingly treated as causation

Mistake: The institute studies migration. Therefore, its logo depicts a flock of birds.

Failure: The logo is suitable for the institute’s purpose, but the sentence does not establish a causal inference.

Repair: The institute studies migration. Fittingly, its logo depicts a flock of birds.

Portable rule: Require an appropriateness match for fittingly.

12. Comma splice with however

Mistake: The signal weakened, however, the receiver still detected it.

Failure: However does not turn a comma into a legal independent-clause boundary.

Repair: The signal weakened; however, the receiver still detected it.

Portable rule: Solve the clause boundary separately from transition meaning.

Anki-style ending

One-page recall deck

Each front restates a taught rule. Each back contains a complete sentence, answer-like form, and compact explanation or contrast.

Card 1 Finite verb slot

Every independent clause needs a subject and a finite main verb.

Complete sentence: The shoreline measurements were checked twice.

Answer-like form: measurements + were checked

Explanation / contrast: Were checked supplies tense and plural agreement; checked alone could be nonfinite.

Card 2 True-subject agreement

Ignore interrupters and match the finite verb to the head subject.

Complete sentence: The set of preserved seeds is stored in a sealed cabinet.

Answer-like form: set = singular; is = singular

Explanation / contrast: Seeds is nearby but does not control the verb.

Card 3 Time-anchor line

Choose tense and aspect from event order and reference time.

Complete sentence: By 2022, the team had completed three surveys.

Answer-like form: had completed before past reference 2022

Explanation / contrast: Past perfect marks the earlier completed event.

Card 4 Antecedent–role match

A pronoun needs a clear antecedent, agreement, and correct case.

Complete sentence: The analysts presented their findings, and the director thanked them.

Answer-like form: their = possessive plural; them = object plural

Explanation / contrast: Both pronouns clearly refer to analysts.

Card 5 Modifier target lock

Place a modifier beside the element it logically describes.

Complete sentence: Using lidar, the researchers mapped the forest canopy.

Answer-like form: Using lidar -> researchers

Explanation / contrast: Researchers can use lidar; the canopy cannot.

Card 6 Owner–number parse

Form the plural first, then mark possession; expand contractions.

Complete sentence: The two curators’ notes show that it’s a later copy.

Answer-like form: curators’ = plural possession; it’s = it is

Explanation / contrast: Apostrophe location follows owner number and contraction meaning.

Card 7 Role–form pairing

Equal logical roles require matching grammatical forms.

Complete sentence: The study aimed to measure, to compare, and to explain the pattern.

Answer-like form: three parallel infinitives

Explanation / contrast: Each infinitive completes aimed at the same structural level.

Card 8 Relationship before word

Summarize both ideas and name their relationship before choosing a transition.

Complete sentence: The test was brief. Nevertheless, its result was consistent.

Answer-like form: limitation + surviving result = nevertheless

Explanation / contrast: Additionally would lose the concession.

Card 9 Development-job match

Distinguish addition, example, specification, restatement, and emphasis.

Complete sentence: The collection includes several tools. Specifically, it contains three bronze compasses.

Answer-like form: broad category -> exact detail = specifically

Explanation / contrast: For example gives a case; specifically narrows to an exact detail.

Card 10 Cause-direction map

Separate cause, effect, goal, action, and inference.

Complete sentence: The membrane blocked salt ions. Consequently, the output water had lower salinity.

Answer-like form: cause -> effect = consequently

Explanation / contrast: The connector preserves the direction from mechanism to result.

Card 11 Timeline connector

Use a time transition only when the event order supports its exact meaning.

Complete sentence: One team sampled the water. Meanwhile, another recorded air temperature.

Answer-like form: overlapping actions = meanwhile

Explanation / contrast: Eventually would require a delayed or final later event.

Card 12 Transition punctuation

Solve the clause boundary and the connector meaning as separate fields.

Complete sentence: The survey was small; however, its pattern was consistent.

Answer-like form: IC ; however , IC

Explanation / contrast: A comma before however would create a comma splice.
