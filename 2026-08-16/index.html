Tense, Structure & Evidence-Bounded Reasoning

This class connected sentence-level control with passage-level reasoning: first identify what the grammar or evidence permits, then choose the smallest answer that satisfies it.

Class review + 15-question homework

What we covered

  • Sentence Boundaries: repaired mistakes involving comma splices, subject–verb separation, and a colon placed before a modifying phrase.
  • Verb tense: compared present perfect and past perfect through timelines, current effects, completed earlier actions, and time markers such as since and for.
  • Form, Structure, and Sense: used changing forms, controllers, time anchors, and meaning checks to decide what a sentence requires.
  • Critical Reasoning: identified research context, mapped a study’s structure, separated correlation from causation, and limited conclusions to what the evidence supports.

Learning snapshot

What is becoming reliable

  • Recognizing a non-finite verb phrase as a modifier.
  • Explaining has gone to versus has been to.
  • Connecting present perfect to an effect that still matters now.
  • Rejecting a causal connector when the evidence shows only correlation.

Next focus

  • Choose perfect tense independently from the timeline, not from one familiar signal word.
  • Identify observation, comparison, and experiment more quickly.
  • Preserve population, condition, direction, and certainty in every inference.
