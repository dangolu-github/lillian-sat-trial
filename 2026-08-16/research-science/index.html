Research map

See the study before judging the claim

Research passages become manageable when each sentence has a study role.

1. Research texts move through five study fields

Rule. A research passage usually presents a question or hypothesis, a design, a result, an interpretation, and a limit. Not every passage states all five, but never merge their jobs.

Worked field modelResearchers asked whether brief exposure to blue light affects seedling growth. They assigned seedlings to blue-light and white-light conditions, measured stem length after ten days, and found shorter stems in the blue-light group. The finding is consistent with an effect of light condition, although the study did not test mature plants.
Field map: question → design → result → interpretation → limit
Sentence analysisThe first sentence states the research question. The second names assignment, comparison conditions, and the measured response. The third reports the group difference, interprets it cautiously, and limits the population.
MistakeThe researchers proved that blue light reduces growth in all plants.
  • Failure: The statement converts one study result into universal proof and drops the seedling-only boundary.
  • Repair: In this study, seedlings exposed to blue light developed shorter stems than seedlings exposed to white light.
  • Portable rule: Keep each sentence in its study field; do not promote a result into a broader conclusion.

2. A hypothesis predicts a relationship; a result reports what occurred

Rule. A hypothesis is a testable proposed relationship between variables. A prediction states the expected observable pattern if that hypothesis is correct. Neither is a result.

Worked field modelThe team hypothesized that warmer soil accelerates germination; therefore, it predicted that seeds kept at 24°C would germinate sooner than seeds kept at 18°C. The 24°C group germinated an average of 1.8 days sooner.
Field map: hypothesis: warmer soil affects germination | prediction: 24°C sooner than 18°C | result: 1.8 days sooner
Sentence analysisThe hypothesis names the proposed relationship. The prediction translates it into a comparison that can be observed. The final sentence reports the measured outcome without yet explaining why it occurred.
MistakeThe hypothesis found that warmer seeds germinated 1.8 days sooner.
  • Failure: A hypothesis cannot find a result; researchers test a hypothesis and observe a result.
  • Repair: The result was consistent with the hypothesis: seeds at 24°C germinated 1.8 days sooner on average.
  • Portable rule: Track who does each job: a hypothesis predicts; a method tests; a result reports.

3. Variables turn a broad topic into a testable design

Rule. The explanatory variable is changed or compared; the response variable is measured; controlled variables are kept comparable; a confounding variable changes with the explanatory variable and offers another explanation.

Worked field modelTo compare two insulation materials, engineers wrapped identical containers with foam or wool and measured heat loss after thirty minutes. Container size, starting temperature, and room temperature were held constant.
Field map: explanatory = insulation material | response = heat loss | controlled = size, start temperature, room temperature
Sentence analysisMaterial is the factor compared. Heat loss is the outcome measured. The three controlled factors could otherwise change the outcome independently of material.
MistakeBecause the foam containers started 5°C warmer, material was the only explanation for their greater heat loss.
  • Failure: Starting temperature changed with material, so it is a confound rather than a controlled variable.
  • Repair: The greater heat loss may reflect insulation material, starting temperature, or both because the two factors changed together.
  • Portable rule: Name X, Y, and every condition that could change Y before accepting a causal story.

4. A comparison group and a controlled variable are not the same thing

Rule. A comparison or control group provides a baseline condition. A controlled variable is a feature kept comparable across groups. The word control can name either job, so identify the exact role.

Worked field modelOne set of plots received the new fertilizer, while untreated plots served as the comparison group. All plots received the same amount of water, making water amount a controlled variable.
Field map: comparison group = untreated plots | controlled variable = water amount
Sentence analysisThe untreated plots show what happened without the treatment. Equal watering prevents water amount from becoming a competing explanation for a group difference.
MistakeThe untreated plots were controlled variables in the study.
  • Failure: Plots are study units in a comparison condition; the held-constant feature is water amount.
  • Repair: The untreated plots formed the control group, and water amount was controlled across both groups.
  • Portable rule: Ask whether control names a baseline group or a protected condition.

5. Experiment and observation support different claim strengths

Rule. An experiment deliberately assigns or manipulates a condition; an observational study measures naturally occurring differences. Strong manipulation and control make causation more defensible, while observation usually supports association.

Worked field modelA survey found that commuters who cycled to work reported lower stress than commuters who drove. Because participants chose their own travel mode, the study identifies an association but does not isolate travel mode as the cause.
Field map: observational comparison → association; causation not isolated
Sentence analysisTravel mode was observed, not assigned. Work schedule, exercise habits, distance, or prior stress could differ between the groups and help explain the pattern.
MistakeCycling to work caused the surveyed commuters to experience less stress.
  • Failure: The causal verb caused exceeds an observational design with self-selected groups.
  • Repair: Among surveyed commuters, cycling to work was associated with lower reported stress.
  • Portable rule: Match the claim verb to the design: associated with is weaker than caused.
2

Results and limits

Read the evidence without enlarging it

A strong SAT conclusion preserves the exact comparison, degree, population, and uncertainty.

6. Randomization, replication, and sample size protect different claims

Rule. Random assignment reduces systematic group differences; replication shows whether a pattern recurs across independent units or studies; sample size affects how much evidence a result contains. None removes every bias.

Worked field modelThe researchers randomly assigned 120 volunteers to two study conditions and repeated the procedure at three laboratories. The design reduces assignment bias and tests whether the pattern recurs across sites.
Field map: random assignment → comparable groups | replication → recurring pattern | n = 120 → evidence amount
Sentence analysisRandom assignment protects the group comparison. Multiple laboratories test repeatability across sites. The sample size describes the number of volunteers but cannot by itself guarantee representativeness.
MistakeBecause the sample was large, the volunteers represented every adult population.
  • Failure: A large sample can still come from a narrow or biased population.
  • Repair: The sample provides substantial evidence about the recruited volunteers, but wider generalization depends on how they were selected.
  • Portable rule: Do not use one design strength to erase a different design limit.

7. A result is a measured pattern; an interpretation explains its meaning

Rule. Results report observations or numerical relationships. Interpretations connect those results to a hypothesis, mechanism, or larger claim. A result can be accurate even when more than one interpretation remains possible.

Worked field modelMoths exposed to the odor located food 22% faster than unexposed moths. The researchers interpreted this difference as evidence that the odor helped the moths orient toward the food source.
Field map: result = 22% faster | interpretation = odor aided orientation
Sentence analysisThe percentage is the measured comparison. The orientation explanation assigns meaning to that comparison and could be evaluated against alternative mechanisms.
MistakeThe 22% difference was the mechanism that guided the moths.
  • Failure: A numerical result describes the pattern; it does not name the biological process producing it.
  • Repair: The 22% difference was consistent with the proposed orientation mechanism.
  • Portable rule: Separate what the data show from why researchers think the pattern occurred.

8. A quantitative claim needs a complete comparison sentence

Rule. Preserve group, measure, comparison, direction, magnitude, unit, and condition. A true number can still fail if it answers the wrong comparison.

Worked field modelAfter six weeks, treated plants averaged 18.4 cm in height, whereas untreated plants averaged 15.1 cm under the same greenhouse conditions; the treated group was 3.3 cm taller on average.
Field map: treated vs untreated | mean height | +3.3 cm | after six weeks | same greenhouse conditions
Sentence analysisThe statement names both groups, the measured variable, the direction and magnitude of the difference, the unit, the time point, and the shared condition.
MistakeThe treatment increased plant growth by 18.4 cm.
  • Failure: 18.4 cm is the treated group's final mean, not the increase and not the difference between groups.
  • Repair: After six weeks, treated plants were 3.3 cm taller on average than untreated plants.
  • Portable rule: Translate values into the exact comparison before interpreting them.

9. Correlation does not establish causation

Rule. Correlation means two variables vary together. Causation requires evidence that changing one variable produces change in the other while plausible alternatives are controlled.

Worked field modelAcross twelve wetlands, sites with more aquatic plants also contained more frog species. The pattern may reflect habitat complexity, water quality, wetland size, or several factors together.
Field map: plant abundance ↔ frog diversity association | causal factor unresolved
Sentence analysisThe two measures co-vary across sites, but the design does not isolate plant abundance. Multiple site characteristics could produce the same association.
MistakeIncreasing aquatic plants will necessarily create more frog species in any wetland.
  • Failure: The statement converts association into intervention, may into will, and twelve sites into any wetland.
  • Repair: In the twelve surveyed wetlands, greater aquatic-plant abundance was associated with greater frog-species diversity.
  • Portable rule: Association travels with the observed population and conditions; it does not become a universal intervention rule.

10. Uncertainty and precision do not mean that a result is useless

Rule. Measurement uncertainty states a defensible range around an estimate. Precision describes closeness among repeated measurements. Precise measurements can share a systematic offset, and uncertainty does not erase a measured pattern.

Worked field modelA calibrated sensor estimated the sample's mass as 50.02 ± 0.05 g. Repeated readings clustered closely, but the reported range still preserves measurement uncertainty.
Field map: estimate = 50.02 g | uncertainty = ±0.05 g | precision = close repeated readings
Sentence analysisThe estimate and range report what values are reasonably compatible with the measurement process. Close readings show precision; calibration helps address known instrument bias but cannot remove every source of uncertainty.
MistakeBecause the uncertainty was ±0.05 g, the researchers learned nothing about the sample's mass.
  • Failure: A bounded uncertainty range is not complete ignorance and does not cancel the estimate.
  • Repair: The measurement supports a mass near 50.02 g while acknowledging a ±0.05 g uncertainty range.
  • Portable rule: Keep the estimate and its uncertainty together; neither should erase the other.

11. A limitation sets a claim ceiling

Rule. A limitation identifies what the design cannot establish: causation, mechanism, population, condition, duration, or measurement precision. The conclusion must stay below that ceiling.

Worked field modelThe six-week study included only juvenile trees grown indoors. Its results may describe short-term responses under controlled indoor conditions, but they do not establish how mature trees respond over several years outdoors.
Field map: population = juvenile trees | condition = indoors | duration = six weeks | claim ceiling = short-term indoor response
Sentence analysisThe population, setting, and duration each restrict generalization. The study may still provide useful evidence inside those boundaries.
MistakeThe study's limitations prove that its findings are false.
  • Failure: A limit narrows a claim; it does not automatically reverse or invalidate the observed result.
  • Repair: The findings apply most directly to juvenile trees over six weeks in the tested indoor conditions.
  • Portable rule: Use limitations to resize a claim, not to discard evidence automatically.

Official SAT route map

Use the Study Map first; then switch to the exact question method

The domain map organizes the research. The question method controls the answer decision.

Central Ideas and Details

Most useful Study Map fields: Question + Result + Limit + Claim.
SAT method: Topic + So-What.
  • Name the research topic.
  • State the whole text's finding or claim.
  • Check the beginning, main result, and ending limit.
  • Reject topic-only, single-detail, and scope-shift choices.

Pattern: the Study Map prevents one vivid result from replacing the whole passage.

Command of Evidence

Most useful Study Map fields: Design + Result + Claim.
SAT method: Claim-Direction-Evidence Lock.
  • Box the claim and evidence direction.
  • Predict the variable, group, and comparison the evidence must show.
  • For data, cite the exact cells before matching wording.

Pattern: a true number is useful only if it performs the stated claim job.

Inferences

Most useful Study Map fields: Design + Result + Limit.
SAT method: Minimum Safe Inference.
  • List the explicit facts.
  • Mark the design and its unresolved alternatives.
  • Choose the strongest conclusion that adds no unsupported cause, population, or certainty.

Pattern: the closest trap usually keeps the result but deletes one design limit.

Words in Context

Most useful Study Map fields: local Result + Limit + Claim degree.
SAT method: Context-Meaning Lock.
  • Mark the sentence's logical job.
  • Predict a plain local meaning.
  • Test exact meaning, degree, and grammar in the original sentence.

Pattern: scientific verbs often differ more in strength than in positive or negative direction.

Text Structure and Purpose

Most useful Study Map fields: every field and the move between fields.
SAT method: Content-to-Job Map.
  • Paraphrase the target sentence.
  • Map the line before, target line, and line after.
  • Name the action, target, and scope.

Pattern: a sentence can report a new study while functioning to qualify an earlier claim.

Cross-Text Connections

Most useful Study Map fields: a separate Question/Design/Result/Limit/Claim map for each text.
SAT method: Two-Column Claim Map + Response + Reason Predictor.
  • Map each researcher's claim, reason, and limit separately.
  • Box the exact target claim.
  • Predict the responder's polarity, reason, and boundary.

Pattern: two researchers may agree on the result but disagree about what the design can establish.

12. Command of Evidence asks whether evidence performs the exact claim job

Rule. Lock the claim, evidence direction, variables, population, and condition. Evidence that is relevant to the topic can still fail to illustrate, support, or undermine the stated claim.

Worked field modelClaim: urban noise reduces birds' ability to detect alarm calls. Direct support would show poorer alarm-call detection under greater urban noise while preserving the same birds, call type, and comparison conditions.
Field map: claim + support direction + same variables + same population + matched condition
Sentence analysisUseful evidence must test noise level against alarm-call detection. A finding about song volume, migration, or a different species may be interesting but does not perform the requested support job.
MistakeBirds in noisy cities sang at higher frequencies, so the finding directly supports reduced alarm-call detection.
  • Failure: The topic matches, but the measured outcome changes from detection to song frequency.
  • Repair: Birds detected fewer alarm calls during high-noise trials than during otherwise matched low-noise trials.
  • Portable rule: Relevant is not enough; the evidence must move the exact claim in the requested direction.

13. Inferences require the minimum safe conclusion

Rule. Combine the stated facts, preserve every limit, and choose the strongest conclusion that adds no unsupported cause, population, mechanism, or certainty.

Worked field modelIn a field survey, shaded ponds had lower water temperatures and more salamander larvae than unshaded ponds. The evidence supports an association among shade, temperature, and larval abundance in the surveyed ponds; it does not isolate which factor caused the difference.
Field map: facts + observed relationship + population/condition + cautious modality
Sentence analysisThe minimum conclusion keeps the three observed variables and the surveyed-pond scope. It does not select shade or temperature as the cause without experimental control.
MistakeAdding shade will increase salamander populations in every pond.
  • Failure: The statement adds an intervention, a causal direction, a long-term population outcome, and a universal scope.
  • Repair: In the surveyed ponds, shaded conditions were associated with cooler water and greater larval abundance.
  • Portable rule: Every extra noun, cause, time frame, and certainty word needs evidence.

14. Text Structure and Purpose asks what a research stage does

Rule. Content tells what a sentence says; function tells what it does. In research texts, common jobs include introduce a question, describe a method, report a result, qualify an interpretation, or identify a limitation.

Worked field modelPrevious surveys linked canopy loss with declining insect diversity. However, those surveys measured only one season. This limitation sentence qualifies the earlier evidence by narrowing its time scope.
Field map: qualify + earlier evidence + one-season time limit
Sentence analysisThe target sentence does not reject the surveys or present a new result. It changes how broadly the earlier association may be interpreted.
MistakeThe sentence summarizes the surveys' finding that insect diversity declined.
  • Failure: The content is about one-season measurement, and its job is to qualify the prior evidence, not restate the decline.
  • Repair: The sentence qualifies the earlier association by identifying a limitation in study duration.
  • Portable rule: Name an action and its target: qualify what, support what, contrast what, or explain what.

15. Words in Context preserves scientific tone and degree

Rule. Use the surrounding logic to recover denotation, tone, degree, and grammatical role. Research verbs and qualifiers such as suggests, corroborates, accounts for, and qualifies are not interchangeable.

Worked field modelA second laboratory obtained the same pattern, thereby corroborating the original finding. Here, corroborating means providing independent support, not proving the explanation beyond doubt.
Field map: corroborating = independently supporting | positive evidence | not absolute proof
Sentence analysisThe second laboratory repeats the pattern, so the context requires a support meaning. The sentence does not establish that every proposed mechanism is correct.
MistakeCorroborating means permanently proving every part of the original hypothesis.
  • Failure: The definition is too absolute and enlarges a repeated pattern into total proof.
  • Repair: The second result corroborates, or independently supports, the original finding.
  • Portable rule: Scientific vocabulary often controls claim strength; preserve degree as carefully as topic.

Claim language

Use verbs that match the evidence

Scientific wording controls how much the sentence claims.

Language job Complete legal sentence Closest illegal overclaim
reports
states a result		 The study reports that treated plants were 3.3 cm taller on average. The study reports why the treatment produced every observed difference.
suggests
supports cautiously		 The pattern suggests that light condition may affect early stem growth. The pattern suggests with certainty that light controls all plant growth.
is consistent with
fits but does not isolate		 The result is consistent with the proposed orientation mechanism. The result is consistent with the mechanism, so no alternative explanation remains.
corroborates
adds independent support		 A second laboratory corroborated the original finding by obtaining the same pattern. A second laboratory corroborated every possible explanation of the pattern.
qualifies
narrows a claim		 The one-season design qualifies the conclusion by limiting its time scope. The one-season design qualifies the result as false.
demonstrates
shows strongly within tested bounds		 The controlled trial demonstrates a difference between the two tested conditions. The controlled trial demonstrates that the treatment works for all populations and conditions.

Overall method

Study Map: Question · Design · Result · Limit · Claim

Complete these five fields before judging an answer about a research passage.

1 · QuestionWhat relationship is being tested?
2 · DesignWhat is compared, controlled, assigned, or observed?
3 · ResultWhat exact pattern occurred?
4 · LimitWhat remains untested or uncertain?
5 · ClaimWhat is the strongest safe conclusion?

Full Study Map model

Pattern to notice: complete all five fields before reading answer choices; the limit controls the final claim strength.

Researchers randomly assigned greenhouse ferns to mineral-fertilizer or organic-fertilizer conditions. All ferns received equal water and light. After four weeks, the mineral-fertilizer group produced 14% more leaf area on average. Because the study tested one species in greenhouse conditions for four weeks, longer-term outdoor effects remain unknown.

Field map: randomized comparison + matched water/light → +14% leaf area at four weeks → one species/greenhouse/time limit → cautious tested-condition claim
Resolved method trace

Question: Does fertilizer type affect leaf growth?

Design: Ferns were randomly assigned to mineral or organic fertilizer; water and light were held constant.

Result: The mineral group produced 14% more leaf area after four weeks.

Limit: One fern species, greenhouse conditions, four weeks.

Claim: For the tested ferns and conditions, mineral fertilizer was associated with greater four-week leaf growth; broader or longer effects remain untested.

Resolved complete-context demonstrations

One research map, six different SAT decisions

Each model gives you a different classroom move: the passage stays stable, but the question job and trap change.

Complete passage and data · experimental recovery study

Marine ecologist Alina Serrano and her colleagues investigated how quickly juvenile Olympia oysters resume feeding after a short low-oxygen event. The team randomly assigned 96 hatchery-raised juveniles to either two hours of low-oxygen water or two hours of normal-oxygen water. After all oysters were returned to normal oxygen, the researchers measured filtration rate 10 minutes and 60 minutes later.

Mean filtration rate (milliliters per minute)
Condition 10 minutes 60 minutes
Normal oxygen 5.2 5.4
Low oxygen 2.9 5.1

Serrano's team concluded that the short low-oxygen exposure temporarily reduced feeding in these juveniles but that their average filtration rate nearly recovered within an hour. The study did not test survival or the biological mechanism of recovery. Later, a second laboratory used adult oysters from a different coastal population under the same oxygen conditions and found that the low-oxygen group still filtered 28% less than its control group after 60 minutes. That later result tempered any broad claim that rapid recovery occurs across oyster ages and populations. Because age and source population changed together between studies, however, the comparison did not identify which factor accounted for the different recovery pattern.

Study Map: Question = recovery after short low oxygen · Design = randomized juvenile experiment, followed by a second study with adults from another population · Result = juvenile gap shrank from 2.3 to 0.3 mL/min; adult gap remained 28% · Limit = survival and mechanism untested; age and population confounded across studies · Claim = rapid recovery occurred in the tested juveniles but should not be generalized across ages or populations.

Demonstration 1 · Central Ideas and Details

Question job: whole-text central idea Controlling fields: Result + Limit + Claim
Strategy trained: use the ending limit to stop one strong result from becoming the whole main idea.
Cue map: juvenile randomized result → near recovery → adult/population contrast → unresolved age-versus-population limit.

Which choice best states the main idea of the passage?

A Short low-oxygen events permanently reduce feeding in oysters of all ages and populations.
B A randomized juvenile study found near recovery after a temporary feeding disruption, but a different result in adults from another population limits broader generalization without isolating the reason for the difference.
C Serrano's team determined the biological mechanism that allows juvenile oysters to survive low-oxygen events.
D The second laboratory reproduced the juveniles' recovery pattern and thereby confirmed that age and population do not affect feeding recovery.
Resolved decision

Choice B

Topic + So-What: Topic = oyster feeding recovery after low oxygen. So-what = tested juveniles nearly recovered, but the later adult/population result narrows the claim. Whole-text scope = preserve both findings and the unresolved cross-study confound.
Closest trap: D.
  • A: changes temporary to permanent and tested groups to all oysters; time and population scope fail.
  • B: preserves the result, contrast, and unresolved limit.
  • C: claims a mechanism and survival finding the study explicitly did not test.
  • D: recognizes replication language but reverses the second result and erases the age/population limit.
Portable pattern: a central idea must include the passage's important turn, not just the first result.

Demonstration 2 · Command of Evidence

Question job: match a claim to data Controlling fields: Group + measure + comparison + magnitude
Strategy trained: label the two group gaps before choosing; a true change within one group may still be incomplete evidence.
Cue map: test the claim that the juvenile low-oxygen group recovered most, but not all, of its initial filtration-rate gap.

Which choice most effectively uses data from the table to support that claim?

A The normal-oxygen group's mean filtration rate rose from 5.2 to 5.4 mL/min.
B The low-oxygen group's mean filtration rate increased by 2.2 mL/min between 10 and 60 minutes.
C At 10 minutes, the low-oxygen group's mean was 2.3 mL/min below the normal-oxygen group's mean.
D The low-oxygen group's deficit relative to the normal-oxygen group narrowed from 2.3 mL/min at 10 minutes to 0.3 mL/min at 60 minutes.
Resolved decision

Choice D

Claim-to-Cells Map: Claim = most of the gap disappeared, but a smaller gap remained. Required cells = both groups at both times. Comparisons = 5.2−2.9 = 2.3 and 5.4−5.1 = 0.3. D states both the narrowing and the remaining difference.
Closest trap: B.
  • A: is true but describes only the control group; claim-variable match fails.
  • B: shows recovery within the low-oxygen group but does not prove how much of the between-group gap remained.
  • C: gives the initial gap only; time comparison is incomplete.
  • D: matches groups, measure, both times, direction, and magnitude.
Portable pattern: when a claim contains both change and comparison, the evidence must contain both.

Demonstration 3 · Inferences

Question job: minimum safe conclusion Controlling fields: Design + cross-study limit
Strategy trained: notice when two variables change together; the result may challenge a broad claim without identifying the cause of the difference.
Cue map: second study changed oyster age and source population at the same time.

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the passage?

A Adult age alone caused the slower recovery observed by the second laboratory.
B The second laboratory's finding invalidates the filtration measurements from the juvenile experiment.
C The rapid recovery observed in the tested juveniles may not extend to other ages or populations, and the available comparison cannot separate those two possible influences.
D Juveniles from the second coastal population would necessarily show the same recovery pattern as Serrano's hatchery juveniles.
Resolved decision

Choice C

Minimum Safe Inference: Facts = different recovery result + adults + different population. Limit = age and population changed together. Minimum conclusion = broad recovery claim is uncertain; causal attribution to either factor remains unresolved.
Closest trap: A.
  • A: keeps the observed difference but promotes one confounded factor into the isolated cause.
  • B: treats a generalization limit as if it erased the original measured result.
  • C: preserves cautious modality, populations, and the unresolved comparison.
  • D: predicts an untested group with certainty.
Portable pattern: a conflicting result can lower claim scope without proving why the studies differ.

Demonstration 5 · Text Structure and Purpose

Question job: sentence function Controlling fields: first Claim → second Result → new Limit
Strategy trained: translate a sentence's content into its job in the research argument.
Cue map: compare the juvenile conclusion before the target sentence with the broader claim after it.

Which choice best describes the function of the sentence beginning “Later, a second laboratory” in the passage?

A It identifies the biological mechanism responsible for reduced feeding under low oxygen.
B It restates Serrano's juvenile result using a larger sample from the same population.
C It introduces the original research question before the experimental design is described.
D It presents a contrasting replication that motivates a narrower recovery claim while creating an age-versus-population interpretive limit.
Resolved decision

Choice D

Three-Line Function Map: Before = juvenile near-recovery conclusion. Target = different result in adults from another population. After = broad claim is tempered, but cause of study difference remains unresolved. Function = qualify the earlier claim through contrasting replication.
Closest trap: B.
  • A: invents a tested mechanism.
  • B: recognizes a second study but reverses its population and result.
  • C: assigns the sentence a job that occurred at the passage's beginning.
  • D: names the action, target, and exact scope consequence.

Complete paired passage · interpreting cross-study evidence

Text 1: Researcher Nia Ruan argues that the second laboratory's adult-oyster result indicates that life stage is an important determinant of feeding recovery after low oxygen. Ruan notes that adult and juvenile oysters differ in energy allocation and says the age difference is the most biologically plausible explanation for the contrasting results.

Text 2: Statistician Elias Kim agrees that the second result makes a universal rapid-recovery claim untenable. Kim argues, however, that Ruan's attribution to life stage exceeds the comparison: the second study changed both age and source population. Kim proposes testing juveniles and adults from both populations under the same protocol.

Two maps: Text 1 = different result + life-stage explanation + biological plausibility. Text 2 = agrees broad claim must narrow + rejects isolated age attribution + crossed age-by-population design. Relationship = agreement on the scope limit, disagreement on what explains it.

Strategy trained: separate agreement about a result from disagreement about the causal explanation.
Cue map: target Ruan's life-stage attribution, not the shared view that the broad recovery claim must narrow.

Based on the texts, how would Kim in Text 2 most likely respond to Ruan's claim in Text 1?

A He would agree that the studies establish age as the cause because the adult group recovered more slowly.
B He would reject the adult-oyster result as irrelevant because it did not reproduce Serrano's juvenile finding.
C He would agree that the result limits a broad recovery claim but argue that assigning the difference to age alone exceeds a comparison in which population also changed.
D He would argue that additional testing is useful even though the existing comparison already rules out population as an explanation.
Resolved decision

Choice C

Response + Reason Predictor: Target = age explains the different result. Kim's polarity = qualify/disagree with attribution. Reason = age and population changed together. Boundary = he still agrees the broad rapid-recovery claim is unsupported.
Closest trap: D.
  • A: matches the observed direction but ignores Kim's confound objection.
  • B: reverses Kim's use of the result to limit the broad claim.
  • C: matches target, polarity, reason, and shared boundary.
  • D: mentions more testing but contradicts the reason Kim says it is needed.
Portable pattern: predict both the response and the reason; polarity alone cannot distinguish C from a close trap.

Trap library

High-frequency research-text traps

Each trap corrupts one exact field in the Study Map.

Study-field swap

MistakeThe method concluded that the treatment was effective.
  • Failed field: A method describes what researchers did; it does not itself conclude.
  • Repair: Using the stated method, the researchers found that the treated group improved more than the comparison group.
  • Portable rule: Keep method, result, and conclusion in separate roles.

Topic-match trap

MistakeA finding about bird song supports a claim about alarm-call detection because both involve sound.
  • Failed field: The topic overlaps, but the measured outcome does not match the claim.
  • Repair: Support must compare noise level with alarm-call detection under matched conditions.
  • Portable rule: Match variables and evidence job, not topic words.

Association-to-cause trap

MistakeOlder walls caused more lichen because older walls had more lichen.
  • Failed field: The repeated wording reports an association but supplies no causal control.
  • Repair: Wall age was associated with lichen abundance in the surveyed walls.
  • Portable rule: A correlation cannot supply its own causal explanation.

Sample-to-population trap

MistakeResults from 40 coastal trees apply to every tree species in every climate.
  • Failed field: The claim drops species, location, climate, and sample boundaries.
  • Repair: The findings apply most directly to the 40 coastal trees studied under the tested conditions.
  • Portable rule: Carry population and condition into the conclusion.

Result-to-mechanism trap

MistakeA faster response time proves which neural process produced the response.
  • Failed field: The measured outcome does not identify the internal mechanism unless the design tested it.
  • Repair: The faster response is consistent with the proposed process but does not isolate it.
  • Portable rule: Outcome and mechanism are different fields.

Uncertainty-erases-result trap

MistakeBecause an estimate has a range, no conclusion can be drawn.
  • Failed field: A bounded range expresses what is known and unknown; it does not delete the estimate.
  • Repair: State the estimate together with its uncertainty and keep the conclusion within that range.
  • Portable rule: Preserve estimate and uncertainty together.

True-number wrong-comparison trap

Mistake18.4 cm was the treatment effect because treated plants averaged 18.4 cm.
  • Failed field: The final group mean is mistaken for a change or between-group difference.
  • Repair: The treatment-group mean was 18.4 cm; the between-group difference was 3.3 cm.
  • Portable rule: Label every number before using it.

Limit-means-false trap

MistakeA six-week duration makes the observed six-week result false.
  • Failed field: Duration limits later generalization but does not reverse the observation.
  • Repair: The result describes six weeks; longer-term effects remain untested.
  • Portable rule: A limitation narrows; it does not automatically negate.

The hypothesis linked temperature with germination; the prediction expected earlier germination at 24°C; the result was 1.8 days sooner on average.
Field map: proposed relation → expected pattern → measured pattern

Material was compared, heat loss was measured, container size was held constant, and starting temperature became a confound when it differed by material.

Cycling was associated with lower reported stress among surveyed commuters, but self-selection prevented a clean causal claim.

Moths exposed to the odor located food 22% faster; researchers interpreted the difference as evidence of improved orientation.
